How ASK is Assessed at AirGen

We use continuous, competency-based evaluation mirroring international airline standards:

  1. Instructor observation

    Real-time feedback on professional demeanor and cockpit communication.

  2. Scenario exercises

    Realistic in-flight emergency and diversion decision-making challenges.

  3. Mental arithmetic tests

    Time, fuel burn, descent profile, and crosswind calculations without calculators.

  4. Progress reviews

    One-on-one evaluations to identify strength areas and weak spots early.

Understanding Area 100 KSA (EASA)

Area 100 KSA (Knowledge, Skills, and Attitude) is the official EASA competency framework embedded inside ATPL theory. It assesses how you apply technical knowledge, solve complex problems, communicate with ATC and crew, manage workload and handle threats and errors (TEM). It is not a separate authority written paper — it is evaluated progressively through scenario-based classroom sessions.

AirGen integrates the Area 100 KSA philosophy across all DGCA, FAA and EASA syllabi so that every trainee develops genuine airline-level competency.

Common questions

Is ASK a separate subject?

No. It is an overarching competency framework built directly into every single subject, CBT test, and mentor review.

Is Area 100 KSA a standalone EASA exam?

No. It is assessed progressively during your ground school course, and meeting the required standard is mandatory before final ATPL exam recommendation.

Why is mental maths included?

Airlines require pilots to quickly estimate fuel remaining, top-of-descent points, time-to-station, and to cross-check aircraft automation to catch anomalies instantly.

How will I know how I am doing?

You receive one of three objective results at each review: Standard Achieved, Improvement Recommended, or Further Development Required, paired with actionable mentor guidance.
