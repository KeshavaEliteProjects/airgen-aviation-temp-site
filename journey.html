    Stage 2: Training (Competency-Based Ground School)

    Stage 2 Training: Blending ASK competencies into DGCA, FAA and EASA.

    "Most academies teach subjects. AirGen trains pilot competencies — Attitude, Skills, and Knowledge."

  3. How do I become an airline captain?

    Stage 3: Airline career and command

    Stage 3 Destination: From ATPL graduate to first officer and captaincy.

    From ATPL graduate to airline first officer to commander in the left seat. Building the leadership, technical mastery, and operational resilience needed for commercial airline selection.

Inside Stage 3: The Airline Career Path

The goal is not just a pilot licence. It is to build the core competencies needed to progress from cadet to senior captain.

  1. Airline readiness assessment

    Technical knowledge fundamentals, communication, mental maths, situational awareness, decision making, problem solving, professional attitude, CRM and teamwork.

  2. Airline selection preparation

    Technical: aircraft systems, jet performance, flight planning, meteorology, navigation, air law. Non-technical: HR interview, command potential, leadership, communication, stress and workload management.

  3. Assessment centre preparation

    Aptitude, numerical, verbal and spatial reasoning, technical tests, multi-tasking, group discussions and communication exercises, competency-based interviews.

  4. Simulator and type-rating readiness

    SOP discipline, checklist philosophy, cockpit procedures, CRM, TEM, automation awareness and workload management, so you do not start from zero in the sim.

  5. First officer development

    Cadet to junior first officer to first officer: operational discipline, line-oriented flight training (LOFT), SOP adherence and continuous learning.

  6. Command development

    First officer to senior first officer to command: airline leadership, multi-crew management, operational risk assessment, conflict management, mentorship and safety leadership.
